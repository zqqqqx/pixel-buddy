# Pixel Buddy – brand

<img src="wordmark.svg" width="360" alt="Pixel Buddy">

## Name

- **Pixel Buddy** is the product (repository, firmware, install page, releases).
- **Pixel** is the character: his name, what you call him ("Pixel!"), what he calls himself. Renaming him (another
  wake word) changes only the character – the product stays Pixel Buddy.
- In text: "Pixel Buddy"; in the wordmark lower case: "pixel buddy".

## Tagline

> **A little buddy with big eyes.**

## Logo

Two eyes on a dark screen, both looking up and to the right – curious, never quite symmetrical: the right eye is a
little bigger and sits higher (the way Pixel himself always looks a bit askew).

Don't stretch, recolour or put the logo on a busy background. Minimum size 16 px.

## Colours

| Name | Hex | For |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#11141C` | logo ground, dark surfaces |
| Night | `#0B0D12` | background (display, dark app) |
| Cream | `#F6F3EA` | eye white, text on dark, light app background |
| Pixel blue | `#3D6BFF` | iris, "pixel" in the wordmark, main colour |
| UI blue | `#5B8CFF` | buttons and links on dark (a lighter Pixel blue) |
| Lilac | `#A87BFF` | "buddy" in the wordmark, second accent |
| Blush | `#FF689C` | blushing, hearts, accents |
| Mint | `#38D39F` | all good, status ok |

**No gradients.** Surfaces, buttons, bars and the logo are flat – clear like a small screen.

## Type

[Quicksand](../fonts/) (SIL OFL) everywhere: rounded like Pixel's eyes. Bold (700) for "pixel", titles and labels,
Medium (500) for "buddy" and body text. The display gets anti-aliased glyphs in four sizes and the app gets woff2
subsets, both generated by `tools/fontgen.py` and served by the robot, so nothing loads from the internet.

## Interface

- Icons are line icons, never emojis.
- Few words. A label names the thing ("Name", "Language"), no subtitles explaining it.
- Rounded cards and pill buttons, dark night theme and a light cream theme.
- Status as a dot or a word, not a sentence.

## Voice

Pixel talks like a good friend: warm, short, a bit of wit – never preachy, never childish. The app follows him:
short words that say what happens ("Listening", "Saved"). He explains problems with his eyes, not with error
messages.

## Images

Eye images come from the simulator (`sim/`, the same renderer as the display) – see `docs/`. Dark background, big
eyes, no invented frames around the screen. Screenshots of the app use demo data, never real chats.
